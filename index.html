What the council is

The FirstAlt Product Council is a multi-agent system that runs inside Claude Code. You bring a problem, an idea or a proposal. A Product Lead orchestrator frames it, asks you what the context cannot answer, calls the panels that matter, brings their questions and data needs back to you, makes them challenge each other, and ends with a short decision, an iteration path and, if you want one, a clean analysis document.

Without itWith it
  • Decisions reflect whoever was in the meetingSix independent panels, chosen for the question
  • Expert review takes days to scheduleMinutes for a quick lens, one sitting for a full review
  • Evidence lives in workbooks, decks and headsOne knowledge base with sources on every fact
  • Discussion ends in "let's think about it"An explicit verdict with conditions, a first step and a path
  • Decisions rest on opinionEvery panel names the figures it needs, and the pick says what each one would change
  • The thinking is lost after the meetingAn analysis document, kept current through the chat, that you can share and build on
  • The same mistakes repeatEach correction becomes a rule the system follows
What it is not

It does not replace user research, and its simulated users produce hypotheses to test rather than findings. It does not own decisions. People do. It is only as good as the context you give it, and it says so when a fact is missing rather than inventing one.

How a decision gets made

Six steps from your question to a decision you can defend. Simple questions stop after the first answer.

  1. You askA question, a problem, a proposal or a file to ingest.
  2. The orchestrator looks it up, then asksIt separates problem from solution and looks up everything your request names, in the knowledge base and in past sessions. It tells you what it already knows, then asks only about the gaps, each question with a default.
  3. Panels answer, then ask backEach panel thinks in its own context and judges the idea for users, the business and stakeholders. Their questions and the figures they need are checked against the knowledge base, and only what it cannot answer comes back to you before any option is written.
  4. Disagreements are forced into the openPanels that conflict get each other's argument and must hold, revise or concede.
  5. You get a short decisionTwo or three different approaches, each with a verdict, one named pick, an iteration path from the smallest viable version, what data would flip it, and a diagram when the pick changes how something works. Saved to the repository, with an analysis document if you want one.
  6. The system learnsCorrections become rules. Decisions become records whose outcomes you track later.

Why you can trust the answer

The council is only as good as its evidence, so it is strict about where every statement comes from.

  1. Every fact has a sourceA claim the decision rests on carries the source's own words, and a check confirms each quote really appears in the file it cites.
  2. It says when it does not knowA missing fact is reported as "not in context", never filled with a guess.
  3. Conflicts come to youWhen two sources disagree, the work stops and you choose. The choice is recorded.
  4. Simulated means simulatedUser Panel answers are hypotheses to test with real users, and always labelled.
