Cost tiers

The orchestrator picks the cheapest tier that can answer and says which it chose. Say "go deeper" to escalate.

Session flow

Every Tier 2 and 3 session follows this order and ends in options with a pick and a path. Tier 0 and 1 stop after step one with a short answer.

  1. TriageOne line: the mode it inferred and the tier it picked.
  2. Scout your input first, aloneThe scout looks up everything the request names and returns the facts with quotes, plus what the context already says on each intake topic. The orchestrator gates them and sends a thin pack back once.
  3. IntakeWhat I already know, then questions only on the gaps, each with a default, plus any conflict the decision depends on. Say "go" to use the defaults.
  4. Brief and panelsThe brief carries the facts, your answers and the named gaps. Panels answer in parallel without seeing each other, each ending with users, business and stakeholders, a viable first step, its questions and the data it needs.
  5. Panel questions and dataThe panels' questions and figures are checked against the context. What is left, up to five questions and one data table, comes back to you. Your answers and figures are tested against the emerging pick.
  6. Options, a pick and a pathTwo or three different approaches, do nothing included, a verdict each, one pick, its iteration path, and what would flip it. A short reply; the detail is in the record.
  7. DiagramsWhen the pick changes how something works, every flow it touches is drawn on one page.
  8. Save, then keep it currentThe hook checks every quote before you see "saved". If you say yes to an analysis document, it is updated after every later turn.

Questions, data and analysis documents

A decision is only as good as what the council knows. So at Tier 2 and 3 it asks twice, asks for numbers, and leaves you a one-page document you can share and build on. Every question is a click-to-answer prompt with the default first and marked recommended, and you can always type your own answer.

Questions you can understand

Every question follows the same writing rules as the answers: one decision per question, in your words, self-contained, under 25 words, with two to four real options, the default first. A check runs before every question and sends an unclear one back to be rewritten, so a question that mentions tiers, panels, coverage labels or files, or asks two things at once, never reaches you.

Two questions the council used to ask, and how it asks them now.
BeforeAfter
"Can you confirm the PARTIAL coverage on problem and who has it?""Is this about every parent, or mainly parents of special-needs students?"
"Should we go with B and also check consent before texting?"Two questions: "Should parents get a text when a call goes unanswered?" and "Do parents have to agree before we text them?"

Two rounds of questions

The three replies of a Tier 2 or 3 session. Say "go" to any round to use the defaults, or pass --fast.
ReplyWhat you get
1. IntakeWhat I already know, with sources. Then up to four numbered questions on what the context cannot answer: the problem and who has it, the outcome and why now, the data you have, constraints. Each has a default. Skipped when nothing is missing
2. Panel questions and dataWhat the panels see so far, what the context answered for them, up to five remaining questions (who asked, why, the default), and one data table
3. DecisionThe pick, the options, the path, data still needed, open questions, one next step. Under 300 words

Data decides

Each panel names up to two figures it needs. The council asks for them in one table. It never estimates a missing figure, never writes a query for you, and writes a threshold only when you or the context supply one.

An example data request.
FigureWhy it mattersLikely whereIf it comes back...
Driver changes per week on alt trips, last full semesterSizes the problemTrip Services reportingLow: do nothing likely wins. High: the build options earn their cost

A pick that waits on data says "provisional until" the figure. When you paste a number, the reply says whether it confirmed the pick, changed a condition, or flipped it.

Analysis documents

After the first decision in a chat, the council asks once, as a click-to-answer question, whether to save an analysis document. Yes writes analysis/<slug>.md and rewrites it after every later turn in that chat. It is one page, 500 words at most, and the save check refuses more. The full detail stays in the session files, which have no limit. Continue in a new chat with /council --analysis <slug> <next question>.

What an analysis document holds. It names no files, IDs, evidence tags, personas or panels, and the save hook checks.
SectionWhat it says
SummaryThe recommendation, how sure we are, and what would change it, in two or three sentences
ProblemWho has it, how often, how we know
Who it affectsUp to four groups, today against the recommendation
OptionsEach approach with its verdict and one line of why, do nothing included
RecommendationThe pick, its main condition, and the Now, Next, Later path
Still open and changesUp to five open questions or missing figures, and the last five changes

The PRD

/draft prd builds a PRD from the decision record and the analysis document, in twelve fixed sections. Its body is clean like the analysis document, so it reads well in Confluence. The evidence stays in the decision record.

The PRD sections, in order.
SectionAnswers
ProblemWhat business problem are we solving?
Business needWhy is this important now?
Expected outcomeWhat measurable outcome are we expecting?
KPIBaseline and target
Background and contextCurrent process (as-is), customer and user pain points, business drivers, regulatory drivers

AssumptionsWhat we take as true, and how it will be checked

Functional requirementsWhat the product must do
Non-functional requirementsHow well it must do it
Open questionsWhat must be answered before or during build

Evidence, options and handoffs

The three rules that decide whether a council output can be trusted and used.

Evidence has to be quoted

The four citation forms and what each one actually proves.
FormWhat it proves
[V: path]The file exists. Acceptable only for background with no specific claim attached
[V: path "verbatim words"]The file says this. Required for anything the recommendation rests on
[I] claim, because reasoningAn inference with its reasoning stated. An [I] with no reasoning is an assertion wearing a tag
[U]Not in context. The honest answer, and the orchestrator names it as an unassessed risk

The save hook checks every quote against its file automatically and sends a failure back to be fixed before you see the result. By hand: python3 scripts/check_citations.py <file>. Full rules in rules/evidence.md.

Options, not one verdict

Tier 2 and 3 return two or three options, each a genuinely different approach, each with its own verdict, and one named pick. "Do nothing" is always on the table with its cost stated, and the record says what would make you switch to the runner-up. Three sizes of one approach is one option, not three. The pick carries an iteration path: what ships now, what it proves, and what comes next.

The decision record opens with an at-a-glance table and maps field by field onto the twelve PRD sections, so /draft prd transforms it rather than rewriting it.

IDs carry their titles

OI-261005-H8 tells a reader nothing, so no output prints an ID alone. The save hook appends a legend resolving every ID to its title and file; python3 scripts/resolve_ids.py sessions/ --check finds bare ones.

Handing a decision to the design project

/handoff writes one brief per option, so the design project produces variants per approach instead of one blend. Each brief links the user flow drawn for that option and ends in one paste-ready block: open the file, copy the block, paste it. No terminal, no credentials.

Learning loop

One correction today prevents the same mistake in every future session.

AI produces outputA session, intake or draft.
You correct itIn plain words, as you normally would.
AI records the lessonMistake, root cause and a testable rule.
Next sessions read itActive lessons load at session start.
Rule is promotedInto the skill, rule file or check where it belongs.
Recurrence is trackedA repeat raises the count and strengthens enforcement.

Automatic behavior

These run on their own once hooks are enabled. Checks a script can do are never left to the model's memory. Run python3 scripts/selftest.py to check all of them on a throwaway copy.

The six hooks and what each one does.
WhenWhat happens
Session startRebuilds the index and lessons digest, then briefs you: branch, active lessons, open items, sources not yet ingested, days since the last /check
Before a questionChecks every click-to-answer question is clear: one decision, your words, short, real options. An unclear one goes back to Claude to rewrite
Before any editBlocks changes to existing raw sources (new ones can be saved, so /ingest <url> works), and edits to shared content on main
After any editContext: rebuilds the index. Session or draft: checks every quote. Analysis document or PRD: checks it names no files, IDs, personas or panels, and the analysis fits on one page. Diagram spec: renders the HTML. Failures go back to Claude to fix
After every turnAdds the ID legend to sessions and drafts written that turn, sends Claude back once if a decision was made without asking the analysis question, and logs token usage to metrics/usage/
Before any commitBlocks shared-content commits on main and commits with invalid context frontmatter

Working as a team

  1. Start a branchbash scripts/start_work.sh <topic> creates pm/<you>/<topic> from the latest main.
  2. WorkSessions, intake, drafts and diagrams. IDs carry a date and a random suffix, so nobody's IDs clash.
  3. Open a pull request/open-pr checks quality, ID clashes and conflicts with the latest main, then opens the request.
  4. Review and mergeThe curator reviews shared content. Append-only logs merge automatically.

Branch protection for GitHub, Bitbucket and AWS CodeCommit is covered in CONTRIBUTING.md.
